The key translation the beads-task derivation doesn't make on its own
The beads-task IB derivation (`01_beads.ipynb`) is built entirely on the bead task — a volatile, non-terminating tracking problem. The normative variable L_t never stops; it's stabilized by the hazard rate h, and the optimal capacity-limited machine (§6–7) is a non-absorbing bounded ladder: a leaky accumulator that holds a category forever, updating as evidence arrives.

Your DDM work is the opposite regime: a stationary generative process (fixed drift per trial), and the accumulator is absorbing — it terminates at a bound and that termination is the decision, with RT as the readout. In the beads-task derivation's own terms, this is the h → 0 limit: no discounting, ψ(L) = L, so L_t is unbounded LLR accumulation — which is exactly the log-likelihood-ratio form of the SPRT that Gold & Shadlen (2002, 2007) already established as the normative account of your tasks, and which the DDM implements as its continuum limit.

So the first real theoretical move isn't "apply the beads-task derivation" — it's extend it: replace the non-absorbing stabilizing bound with an absorbing decision bound, and ask what the IB-optimal discrete accumulator looks like when capacity is limited and the process must terminate. That's a genuinely new derivation, but it inherits almost everything from §6–7 of the beads-task derivation (state-diagram machinery, the deterministic-machine search, the convex-hull test for abrupt vs. continuous transitions) — you're swapping the boundary condition, not rebuilding the tool. **Done — see below.**

1. Theoretical link: DDM parameters as a signature of capacity
Once you make that swap, the object you get is a quantized random walk with absorbing barriers — which is not a new invention; it's the classical discrete-time random-walk model that Ratcliff & Smith (2004) already show converges to the DDM as step size → 0 and step rate → ∞. The beads-task derivation's contribution is that it tells you the walk isn't a coarsened approximation of the continuous process — it's what an information-optimal encoder produces under a capacity constraint, with a specific, derivable structure (§2.1–2.2): few states, unevenly spaced, concentrated where (occupancy) × (usefulness) peaks, sharpened before split.

**Update: this extension is now built and validated** (`03_ddm.ipynb`, Parts A–C) — a uniform-ladder baseline, an oracle known-drift optimal placement, and (the part that mattered most) an honest, unknown-drift placement, which overturned the naive expectation: honest IB-optimal placement is *not* more accurate than uniform spacing once the encoder can't cheat by knowing the answer in advance — it's slightly and consistently *worse*, though it does avoid a validity floor uniform spacing runs into. That's a real, checked correction to the picture below, not just an implementation detail — the "capacity cut" that the rest of this section reasons about is now the honest placement, not an oracle.

That gives a principled way to ask what a capacity cut does to standard DDM fits, and the derivation needed to compute it (below) now exists — what's still open is running it:

Bound height a (in units of diffusion noise) is the natural proxy for capacity, because the number of resolvable levels between start and bound is bounded by 2^{I(X;R)}. A low-capacity observer can't be modeled as "the same DDM with smaller a" — it's a DDM whose accumulator takes discrete, unevenly-spaced jumps built from a placement that (per the update above) isn't even accuracy-optimal, so the mapping to a is not identity. The qualitative prediction: capacity changes masquerade as changes in a, but leave a distinguishable residual in RT-distribution shape (`03_ddm.ipynb` Part A already shows this banding directly) that pure bound changes don't produce.
Drift v/k should be comparatively protected. In the beads-task derivation, SNR sets the ceiling and β_c, but capacity sets the number of categories (§3, Fig. 4g–h) — these are separable knobs, with state-count onsets moving <0.06 bits across a 2.5× range of d' (now reproduced directly in `02_triangles.ipynb`). Translated: if session-to-session variability is capacity-driven, it should show up disproportionately in bound/collapsing-bound/urgency-like parameters, much less in the drift/sensitivity parameter k that Fan et al. already track separately from bound height a.
Non-decision time and starting point z are the natural place to look for the "sharpen, then split" structure (§2.1c): the criterion location is fixed early, and what changes with capacity is the steepness of the criterion, not its position (`03_ddm.ipynb` Part B §5 shows this directly for the DDM case) — this predicts collapsing-bound- or urgency-like signatures (progressive sharpening) that aren't driven by a deadline at all, but by capacity.
**Still the concrete open deliverable:** fit a standard DDM to synthetic choice/RT data generated from the honest `03_ddm.ipynb` placement, across a range of k, and see which parameter absorbs the capacity manipulation. The encoder to generate that synthetic data from now exists and is validated; the fitting exercise itself has not been run. This is listed as a placeholder prediction in `03_ddm.ipynb`'s predictions section.

2. Behavioral signatures, and how to fit this with limited trials
Two predictions fall out of the beads-task derivation directly, without new derivation, and are now written up as drafted (not just sketched) predictions in `01_beads.ipynb`'s predictions section:

Confidence/discreteness (§2.4). The sharpest, most literature-distinguishing prediction is that a capacity-limited observer holds a binary or few-category belief with a sharp criterion, not a graded one — "what varies with capacity is how sharp the criterion is, not how graded the belief is." If you have confidence-report data from any of these tasks, this is directly testable: graded-belief accounts (including standard DDM confidence-as-balance-of-evidence) predict smooth covariation of confidence with accumulated evidence/RT; the IB account predicts a step function, with capacity changing the steepness of the step, not its shape. Still open: no confidence-report dataset has actually been checked against this yet.

**Update: confidence reports demoted from primary test.** `01_beads.ipynb` §3.1 now flags that eliciting a graded confidence report plausibly changes the representation being measured — a subject asked to produce a calibrated rating has a reason to encode more precision than the binary choice alone would require, so smooth confidence data can't cleanly distinguish "the account is wrong" from "the probe made the belief less categorical." §3.1 (and its cross-task copies in `02_triangles.ipynb`, `03_ddm.ipynb`, `04_interrogation.ipynb`) now leads with choice/RT-only alternatives instead — run-length/magnitude saturation and psychometric/chronometric curve shape — and treats confidence data as converging evidence at best, checked against a confound test (does performance differ on probed vs. unprobed trials) before being trusted.
Reframing "inefficiency" (§6). The derivation shows that a perfectly optimal recursive observer, scored against the one-shot bound, looks 62–87% "efficient" in the low-capacity range purely from the recursion constraint — not from suboptimality (reproduced directly in `01_beads.ipynb`'s recursion-cost section). This is a warning and an opportunity: any variability you've been attributing to noise/suboptimality in fitted efficiency measures should first be checked against what a capacity-constrained-but-optimal recursive observer would produce. This is exactly the reinterpretation Fan et al. 2018 gestures at when they attribute systematic deviations from reward-maximizing bounds to "satisficing and heuristic principles" rather than poor learning — the IB account offers a third option: neither miscalibration nor heuristic satisficing, but rational allocation of a shared, limited representational budget. Worth checking whether the direction and pattern of their reward-asymmetry deviations lines up with what a capacity-limited bound-setting process would predict, versus their gradient-following satisficing account. Still open: not checked against Fan et al.'s actual data.
On fitting individual/session data with few hundred trials — the practical constraint you flagged — the move is to not estimate the IB frontier from trial data at all. The frontier is a property of the task's generative statistics (hazard, SNR, or drift/noise for stationary tasks), which you already estimate robustly per session via standard DDM fitting. So:

Fit the ordinary generative parameters per session (drift/noise, or hazard, as you already do) — this is the part that needs the trial count, and you already have it working.
Compute the IB frontier for that session's task statistics offline, exactly as `01_beads.ipynb`'s foundations section does — this costs no trials at all, it's a numerical solve.
Fit only where on that frontier the session sits — a 1–2 parameter fit (capacity I(X;R), or equivalently β) — using a likelihood built from the discretized accumulator's predicted choice/RT distribution rather than a continuous-DDM likelihood.
That's a much lower-dimensional fitting problem than a full DDM refit, which is what makes it tractable at session-level trial counts. Hierarchical partial-pooling across sessions/subjects (which this lab already does for DDM parameters) would stabilize it further.

3. Neural predictions
All three still open — not yet checked against any real dataset — and now also written up as placeholder predictions in `03_ddm.ipynb`'s predictions section, referencing this section. Directly checkable against data you already have:

Discreteness in ramping activity. If the represented decision variable is quantized into k states rather than continuous, LIP/FEF ramping activity (Law & Gold; Ding & Gold FEF/caudate work) should show clustering around a small number of firing-rate "rungs" at fixed time bins — beyond what Poisson spiking variability alone predicts — with the number of rungs varying by session/subject in a way that should correlate with the behaviorally-fit capacity parameter from #2.
Discrete attractor structure over continuous line-attractor structure. Population-level analyses (energy-landscape reconstruction, or simpler: multimodality of population state at fixed time-in-trial) should show a small number of discrete stable states in low-capacity sessions, rather than the continuous manifold assumed by standard line-attractor/Wong-Wang-style accounts.
Arousal/state as a capacity knob. This lab already has the Aston-Jones & Gold (2009) and Nassar/Gold pupil-linked work on exactly this bead/change-point paradigm. A very direct prediction: pupil-indexed arousal state should predict the fitted capacity parameter on a session or even epoch basis, with low-arousal periods showing more quantized (step-like) behavior — this connects an existing physiological measure directly to this project's central construct (capacity) without needing new recordings.
The basal ganglia angle (Ding & Gold caudate work on flexible decision rules) is also worth flagging as a mechanism candidate for where discretization is imposed — cortico-basal ganglia gating as the implementation of "collapse continuous cortical evidence into a categorical commit state" — but that's a second-order hypothesis, worth having in mind rather than pursuing first.
